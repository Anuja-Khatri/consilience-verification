\begin{abstract}
I present a deterministic, typed engine that compares scientific claims across theories: each claim is
reduced to typed fields (entity\_type, axis, polarity, value) and a fixed rule-based judge assigns a
correspondence grade or a fork. The judge is reproducible, provable and auditable. I re-express it as a
logic program (verdict equivalence \textbf{1.0}, $n = 213$ pairs), as a first-order theory under an SMT
solver (congruence \textbf{1.0}, $n = 311$ verdicts), with machine-checked proof objects (coverage
\textbf{0.9842}, $n = 316$) and \textbf{0} integrity violations ($n = 316$). On the discovery side the
engine recovers the COGITATE adversarial-collaboration key at \textbf{2 of 3} predictions (revised;
$n = 3$; not significant against a 1000-key permutation null, $p = 0.075$). The eLife synergistic-workspace
key recovery is below chance: \textbf{0 exact / 6 partial of 14} validated rows, empirical $p = 0.981$
($n = 1000$; random keys recover more). The method's limits are first-class results: both key recoveries
are reported with their chance rates, single-pass extraction carries a measured noise floor, and the
extraction front-end under-propagates negation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Comparing scientific claims across competing theories is usually done by hand and by argument. This paper
presents a deterministic engine that does it mechanically and auditably. Each claim is reduced to a small
typed record (entity type, axis, polarity, value), and a fixed rule-based judge compares two claims from
the typed fields alone, returning a correspondence grade or a fork. Because the judge is a function of
typed fields, every comparison is reproducible, and the judge can be re-expressed as a logic program, as a
first-order theory under an SMT solver \cite{z3}, and as machine-checked proof objects. This division of
labour, in which a language model proposes typed candidates and a sound external program disposes, follows
the LLM-Modulo pattern of pairing a generator with verifiers it cannot talk its way past
\cite{kambhampati2024}.

I exercise the engine on a frozen corpus of six theories of consciousness, with bridge extensions that link
active inference \cite{buckley2017} to machine learning (control as probabilistic inference
\cite{levine2018}; predictive coding as an approximation to backpropagation \cite{millidge2022}) and that
span integrated information theory in its current formulation \cite{albantakis2023}; nothing in the method
is specific to that domain. I report two kinds of result. On the discovery side, the engine re-adjudicates
a founding cross-field correspondence record and attempts to recover two held-out, published answer keys,
the COGITATE adversarial collaboration \cite{cogitate2025} and the eLife synergistic-workspace bridge
\cite{luppi2024}, each scored against an explicit permutation chance rate. On the verification side, the
judge's decisions are a transparent, checkable function of the typed fields, and a preregistered
statement-level perturbation suite measures how the extraction front-end behaves under meaning-preserving
and meaning-flipping edits. Throughout, I report the method's limits as first-class results rather than
hiding them: discovery claims carry their chance rates, including an honest below-chance negative;
single-pass extraction carries a measured noise floor; and the extraction front-end's under-propagation of
negation is stated plainly. All figures render from a single numbers-locked registry snapshot.

\section{Discovery}

\subsection{Cross-field re-adjudication}
Re-running a founding physics$\times$neuroscience$\times$contemplative correspondence corpus through the
frozen instrument (v2) and the deterministic judge: of the pairs carrying a recorded prior tier ($n = 7$),
the new grade agrees with the prior at \textbf{0.1429} (1/7, $n = 7$) and downgrades or withdraws at
\textbf{0.8571} (6/7, $n = 7$). Most originally-asserted cross-field correspondences do not survive
re-typing under a fixed codebook; I report this as the method's discipline, not a headline.

\subsection{Unification index}
Ranking persisted node correspondences by a preregistered score (forks-touched $+$ support-cone $+$
axis-reach, weights 1/1/1): Kendall's $\tau$ \cite{kendall1938} rank stability across the weight simplex is
\textbf{0.7165} ($n = 216$ correspondences), moderately weight-sensitive and reported honestly; the
generative grade dominates and is flagged.

\subsection{Held-out key recoveries, with chance rates}
Against the held-out adversarial-collaboration key \cite{cogitate2025}, preregistered in advance
\cite{cogitate_prereg}, the v2 instrument recovers \textbf{2 of 3} predictions (revised gated result
\{2,0,1,9\}; $n = 3$). Outcome-informed disclosure (verbatim): ``iit\_c009 was initially rejected at the
gate; after a sensitivity check showed it was the sole binding claim for P3, it was re-reviewed and
approved on codebook-consistency grounds (matching approved canonical iit\_c025). Original gated result
\{1,0,2,9\} and revised \{2,0,1,9\} are both reported; fully ungated sensitivity \{2,0,1,16\}.'' The
remaining miss (P2, temporal\_profile) is attributed to the instrument: no IIT temporal\_profile claim was
ever extracted (not a gate effect). Chance rate: 1000 random typed-row keys recover $\ge$ the real 2/3 with
empirical permutation \textbf{$p = 0.075$} ($n = 1000$) beside a normal-approx $z = 2.2693$, from the same
recovered-count null; the discrete, right-skewed null makes the $z$ overstate significance, so
\textbf{$p = 0.075$} ($n = 1000$) governs: it does not reach $\alpha = 0.05$ and is suggestive only. The
engine never ingested the key text (holdout clean).

Of \textbf{14 validated bridge rows} \cite{luppi2024} the engine's IIT$\times$GNWT fork/correspondence set
recovers \textbf{0 exact} and \textbf{6 partial} ($n = 14$), misses 8, with \textbf{0} rows unmappable to
any engine axis ($n = 14$; the one vocabulary-gap row in the provisional 16-row build was among the 2
dropped at the format gate as methods, so the validated denominator of 14 carries 0 unmappable). Chance
rate (honest negative): the 6-partial recovery does not beat chance. 1000 random typed-row keys recover
more (null mean 9.2), empirical \textbf{$p = 0.981$} ($n = 1000$), below the null. I report it as found: on
the engine's IIT$\times$GNWT set the eLife bridge's partial recovery is at or below chance.

\section{Verification}

\subsection{Audit method}
A rule-based criterion audit (provenance, quote-support, Newman screen, testability, scale): discovery
survivorship \textbf{1 of 9} ($n = 9$) and sampled-match survivorship \textbf{2 of 30} ($n = 30$); strict,
since most prior discoveries fail at least one criterion.

\subsection{Instrument}
Adopted decoding is \texttt{baseline-single-pass}. One stability estimator is used throughout: the sprint
estimator \textbf{0.25} (95\% CI [0.1713, 0.3496], $n = 88$), a measured noise floor every downstream
contrast is scored against. (The prior Task-5 estimator on the same 80 runs is 0.271, a coarser cluster
unit, reported only for comparison.)

\subsection{The verified judge}
Verdict equivalence \textbf{1.0} ($n = 213$); SMT congruence \textbf{1.0} ($n = 311$), discharged by the Z3
solver \cite{z3}; proof coverage \textbf{0.9842} ($n = 316$), with derivations machine-checked in the
spirit of formalised-mathematics libraries \cite{mathlib}; \textbf{0} integrity violations ($n = 316$).

\subsection{Statement-level contracts: the negation headline}
An OSF-preregistered perturbation suite (DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/5N24M}{10.17605/OSF.IO/5N24M}) with a re-extraction noise-floor
control. Polarity caveat: the claim store is near single-class on polarity (92 asserts / 3 denies), so the
identity-control polarity stability \textbf{0.975} ($n = 40$) and the panel polarity agreement reported
below largely reflect the extractor's default rather than demonstrated polarity reading, which is why the
negation figure is the informative one. Explicit negation flips polarity only \textbf{0.1765} of the time
($n = 34$ valid); double negation restores to asserts \textbf{0.0} ($n = 8$ valid, low-$n$, since the
perturber mostly fails to negate twice); L2 verdict tracking \textbf{0.4189} ($n = 74$). The deterministic
judge is correct given fields; the extraction front-end under-propagates negation. A single-reviewer
hand-check agrees with the automated verifier at \textbf{0.893} ($n = 28$).

\subsection{Obfuscation control}
Obfuscating surface vocabulary leaves fork recovery essentially unchanged (gap \textbf{0.072}, $n = 14$),
but the extractor re-identifies the theories and emits their coined terms (\textbf{12} de-obfuscation
leaks, $n = 14$): surface obfuscation does not reduce fork recovery and does not blind the model to theory
identity.

\subsection{Reliability framing}
A two-vendor panel (gpt-4.1 $\times$ gemini-3.1-pro-preview, full coverage) measures protocol
reproducibility, not validity: cross-vendor entity $\kappa$ \cite{cohen1960} \textbf{0.525} ($n = 95$),
axis $\kappa$ \textbf{0.503} ($n = 95$), polarity PABAK \cite{byrt1993} \textbf{0.811} ($n = 95$); the
polarity PABAK carries the single-class caveat noted for the negation headline above. Standing
self-consistency is the adopted stability \textbf{0.25} (sprint estimator, 95\% CI [0.1713, 0.3496],
$n = 88$); the prior Task-5 estimator on the same runs is 0.271.

\section{Limitations}
These limitations import the pre-sprint evaluation's, which that report records and this paper supersedes
under the lock.

The engine's six theories are all theories of consciousness, so results may not transfer to other fields;
cross-field reach rests on the re-adjudication record and the corpus extensions, not the core. The
reference labels were produced by the builder: the judge-after-audit reference reflects one builder's
choices, and a second, independent annotator is planned but not yet run, so human agreement, the ceiling
against which model agreement is read, is not yet available. In production a smaller same-family model
cross-checks the main extractor; the gpt-4.1 $\times$ gemini-3.1-pro-preview panel is an evaluation add-on,
not part of the production pipeline, and it measures reproducibility rather than validity. The corpus is
small (95 approved claims, six theories); the deeper arms, embedding alignment, unsupervised optimal
transport via Gromov-Wasserstein couplings \cite{memoli2011}, and partial-information decomposition of the
verdict \cite{williams_beer2010}, are pilot-scale feasibility studies in a planned second paper.

Single-pass extraction carries a measured noise floor (stability \textbf{0.25}, $n = 88$); every
invariance and tracking number is reported relative to the identity control, and negation tracking is
itself a measured limitation. The store is near single-class on polarity (92 asserts / 3 denies), so
polarity-stability figures reflect the extractor's default rather than demonstrated polarity reading.
Holdout controls the prompt, not the training data: held-out keys never entered extraction or judging
prompts (measured clean), but they may sit in a model's pretraining, and the obfuscation control is the
measured response, which shows that identity is not blinded. Several review packets were judged at the
statement level or from truncated excerpts, without full fork-side source text, so those arms are reported
as ``no fork flagged by the scan'', not as validation of the forks. Decisions were model-drafted
suggestions (Claude) reviewed and confirmed by the author. Finally, the open-weight panel leg is not run (no
hosted-open-weight key), so the panel is two-vendor, stated here as a limitation.

\section{Conclusion}
The engine turns cross-theory claim comparison into a reproducible, auditable computation: a typed
intermediate representation, a deterministic judge, and three independent re-expressions of that judge that
agree exactly. Its discovery claims are modest by construction: most prior cross-field correspondences do
not survive re-typing, one held-out key is recovered only suggestively, and the other is recovered below
chance. I report them that way. The verification lane, not the discovery headline, is the contribution I
stand behind: a claim-comparison verdict that is a checkable function of its inputs.
